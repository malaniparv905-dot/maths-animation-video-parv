1. ROOM & MICROPHONE SETUP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Room Choice       : Choose a quiet, small room with soft surfaces (curtains,
                      bed, carpet, or cushions) to absorb echo.
- Silence Electronics: Turn off fans, AC, fridge hum if possible, and put your
                      phone on Do Not Disturb.
- Mic Distance      : Keep your microphone (or phone/earphone mic) 15–20 cm
                      (~6–8 inches) from your mouth, angled ~30 degrees off-axis
                      so breath bursts ("p", "b", "t") don't pop or distort.
- Recording Format  : Export/save as uncompressed WAV (48 kHz, 16-bit or 24-bit,
                      mono or stereo). If your recorder saves .m4a or .mp3,
                      keep the filename stem identical—the sync script accepts
                      .wav (recommended) and can convert cleanly.

2. AVOIDING CLIPPING & AWKWARD CUTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Silent Padding    : Press Record, WAIT 1 full second in silence, speak the
                      scene cleanly, then WAIT 1 full second in silence before
                      stopping. Never speak immediately as you hit Record!
- Input Gain        : Aim for normal speaking loudness so peaks never hit red
                      (0 dB). Around -12 dB to -6 dB peak is ideal.
- Single Take/Scene : If you stumble on a line, pause 1 second and re-record
                      that short scene file from scratch—each file is only
                      8 to 48 seconds long!

3. SPEAKING PACE & DELIVERY STYLE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Natural Tone      : Imagine explaining this to a classmate sitting next to you—
                      clear, confident, conversational, not reading like a robot.
- Speaking Pace     : Aim for ~130–145 words per minute. Do NOT rush! If your
                      take runs 2–4 seconds longer than the scene window, our
                      synchronization script will automatically hold the clean
                      settled frame instead of speeding up your voice.
- Pauses            : Honor the [PAUSE] cues in Narration_Script.txt. Short
                      pauses let viewers absorb numbers on screen.

4. HOW TO PRONOUNCE MATHEMATICAL EXPRESSIONS NATURALLY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Variables         : Say "x", "y", "z" crisply (and "coffee x, tea y, sandwich z"
                      where written).
- Currency          : Say "five rupees", "eight rupees", "seven rupees" in Scene 01,
                      and short forms ("three, two, five") once context is set.
- Row Operations    : NEVER read symbols robotically ("R two arrow R two minus R
                      one"). Read the written natural phrasing:
                      * "Subtracting Row 1 from Row 2..."
                      * "Multiplying Row 2 by minus one..."
                      * "Dividing Row 3 by two..."
- Negative Numbers  : Say "minus one", "minus three", and "seven minus negative
                      three equals ten" cleanly with a tiny breath before "ten".
- Coordinates       : Pause slightly between numbers: "three, [beat] two, [beat] five".
